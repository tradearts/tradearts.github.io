What it is

An outdoor cabinet on the deck that houses a fridge in one bay and a stacked washer and dryer in the other. It follows the IKEA GLAMBERGET look: a square box with a flush top, two inset sliding doors framed by the cheeks and a head rail, cheeks notched at the bottom so they read as slab legs, and a full-height American walnut stile on each door as the handle. Every panel is black film-faced formply, cut as a flat-pack kit on a CNC router, so assembly needs a drill and a sealant gun and nothing gets cut on site.

How it handles the weather

The pergola above is open, so rain reaches the cabinet. Nothing sits on the deck: ASA feet hold every panel 15 mm up, and the doors hang 75 mm clear on a track hidden behind the head rail. The top is flush, so its edges and all other cut edges get two coats of sealer. The walnut stiles get exterior oil and sealed end grain. The backs are solid. Air moves by itself: cool air comes in through the 75 mm gap under the doors, and the warm air from the fridge coils and the dryer leaves through the meshed slots high in each door, above both appliances.

How it goes together

The cap is routed underneath with 6 mm housings for every vertical. You build the carcass upside down on the cap, so the housings square it up. Then you stand it on the feet, fit the head rail and track, and hang the doors with their walnut stiles. Black aluminium angles stiffen the back corners. The appliances stand on the deck on their own feet; the cabinet carries none of their weight.

Build steps

Press View to see a step highlighted in the model.

  1. Confirm before cutting

    Confirm the washer and dryer models and their stacking kit (dryer must be heat-pump or condenser). Electrician confirms three accessible outlets on the studio wall. Measure the deck, the 201 gap to the slat screen and the 195 gap to the studio wall. Release the two HOLD lead holes, or leave them off the cut.

  2. Seal and print

    Seal every CNC-cut edge with two coats of exterior edge sealer, paying most attention to bottom edges and the vent slots. Print the ASA parts (Prints tab).

  3. Feet on the deck

    Mark the footprint on the deck. Screw the ten ASA feet down with 10g x 40 stainless: two under each cheek, two under the divider, four under the backs. Check they are in one plane with a straightedge; pack under a foot with a shim if a board is low.

  4. Carcass on the cap, upside down

    Lay the top film-face down on padded trestles, housings up. Run PU sealant in each housing. Stand C1, C2, C3, C5 and C6 in their housings; the housings square the box. Screw down through the top into each edge, 8g x 50 stainless at 200 centres, counter-sunk and sealed.

  5. Back corner angles

    Fit the four 25 x 25 x 3 black aluminium angles inside the back corners, sealant in the joint, 8g x 25 stainless at 200 both legs.

  6. Stand it up

    Two people. Turn the carcass upright and lower the panel edges into the feet. Check plumb and diagonals, then screw through each foot into the panel (6g x 20 stainless).

  7. Head rail and track

    Push the head rail up into its housing between the cheeks, flush with their fronts, and screw down through the top. Cut the double top track to 1497 and screw it up into the top behind the head rail.

  8. Walnut stiles and doors

    Machine the two walnut stiles (32 wide, groove 17.5 x 12, finger-jointed or scarfed from offcuts if needed). Oil all faces and seal the end grain. PU-glue each stile onto its door edge and screw through the groove cheeks. Fit the insect mesh behind the high vent groups. Hang D1 on the front track and D2 on the rear; set both doors 75 off the deck.

  9. Floor guide

    Close both doors. Screw the ASA floor guide to the deck where they cross so the fins straddle each door; run both doors end to end and check the stiles pass over the fins.

  10. Grommets and leads

    Only after the HOLD is released: fit the split grommets in the lead holes and route each original lead separately to the wall. No powerboard, nothing in a door track or under a machine foot.

  11. Appliances in

    Roll the fridge in, door hinge on the left, 107 back from the cabinet front. Stand the washer, fit the maker's stacking kit and lift the dryer on. Level all three on their own feet; the cabinet carries none of them.

Open items before the cut

Washer and dryer modelsProvisional 600 x 680 x 850 and 600 x 670 x 850. Confirm both models and the maker's stacking kit before cutting; the dryer must be heat-pump or condenser.
PowerOwner says there is a power point on the studio wall. Position, outlet count and weather cover are unmeasured. The two lead holes are HOLD until an electrician confirms.
WeatherThe pergola is open, so the cabinet takes rain. Film faces are weatherproof; cut edges are the weak point. Seal every edge twice and re-seal any chip. The top is flush (GLAMBERGET look), so its four edges carry the weather: seal them twice and keep an eye on them. Every panel stands 15 off the deck.
Track kitChoose an exterior double top-hung kit rated for two 764 x 1836 doors (about 20 kg each with the stile). It must fit the 45 high x 58 deep zone behind the 70 mm head rail.
Washer doorWith the laundry door open, the parked door and its walnut stile sit 17 mm left of the washer. A left-hinged washer door opened past 90° will touch the stile. Prefer a right-hinged washer, or open it to 90° only.
Walnut outdoorsAmerican walnut is only moderately durable outdoors. Under the open pergola it will grey and needs re-oiling once or twice a year. Seal the end grain at the bottom of each stile.
Site measureFootprint, the 201 gap to the slat screen and the 195 gap to the studio wall come from the handover setout. Measure on site before the cut is released.
Fridge doorShown left-hinged. If it is re-hung to the right, re-check the swing: it would open towards the divider.
