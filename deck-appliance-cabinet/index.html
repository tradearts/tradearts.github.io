What it is

An outdoor cabinet on the deck: a fridge in one bay and a stacked washer and dryer in the other. It reads as a plain black box. The top is flush and the fridge door sits in the same plane as the sides. The black-painted track reads as a 30 mm band under the top. There are no vents on the front. Two short American walnut pulls, cut from workshop offcuts, are the only detail. Every panel is black film-faced formply cut as a flat-pack kit on a CNC router.

Air: what each appliance needs

Fridge: yes, it needs vents. Its condenser is on the back, so it needs air in low and out high, rising past the coils like a chimney. Fridge makers ask for at least 200 cm² of opening at the bottom and at the top when a fridge is built into cabinetry, and the HRBM292S manual asks for 50 mm at the sides and back and 100 mm above. This cabinet gives 72 mm at each side, 186 mm behind and 183 mm above. Each bay has three 210 × 100 louvred openings low and three high in the back panel: 630 cm² per level, about 200 cm² clear once the blades and mesh are allowed for, plus the 10 mm gap under the doors. The back faces the slat screen, so none of it shows.

Dryer: run it with the door open. Heat-pump and condenser dryers recycle the air around them, so in a closed cupboard they heat their own intake air and slow down. Makers either say not to put them in a cupboard or ask for large louvred openings in the cupboard door (LG asks for two of 387 cm², top and bottom). Without front vents the simple answer is to slide the laundry door open while the dryer runs. It parks over the fridge, so nothing gets in the way. The back vents let the bay air out after a run.

Washer: no vents needed while it runs. It makes little heat. Leave its door ajar afterwards so the drum dries; the back vents let the damp out of the bay.

Keeping storm water out of the vents

Each back opening takes a printed ASA louvre insert that sits flush: only a 3 mm flange stands proud of the back. Inside, overlapping blades slope 45° down and out. Each blade is 27 mm deep at a 13 mm pitch, so there is no straight path in, even upwards. Rain that hits the louvre lands on a blade and runs back out. Stainless mesh across the inside stops insects and catches spray. Six openings per bay (three low, three high) make up for the area the blades take, so the fridge still gets about 200 cm² of clear air at each level. The back faces the slat screen, 201 mm away, under cover.

Weather and fixing

The deck is about a metre off the ground under cover, so the box can sit on the deck. It stands on 4 mm ASA pads you can't see from standing height, so water drains out under the ply edges and the deck boards can dry. Every cut edge, including the top's four exposed edges and the door grooves, gets two coats of sealer. Two stainless angles inside the cheeks screw into the deck joists to hold the box down in wind.

How it goes together

The kit is eight sheets: the top, two cheeks, the divider, two backs and two doors. It goes together with one screw type, 52 stainless decking screws (10g × 45, T25 Torx). Every screw goes through a 3.5 mm hole the CNC drills, so the heads land in straight, evenly spaced rows. You pilot 3 mm through each hole into the 17 mm edge behind and drive the screw. All the heads are on the top of the cabinet (1985 up, above eye level) or on the backs (facing the slat screen). There are none in the sides or the front.

The backs now run the full width behind the cheeks and the divider. The kitchen-side back also covers the divider's rear edge, so each back screws straight into the edges in front of it. Only the joint between the two backs needs one black angle inside the laundry bay.

Build it upside down on the top: screw the track in first while it is easy to reach, stand the cheeks and divider in their housings, then screw on the backs, which squares the box. Tip it onto its back to screw down through the top at bench height, then stand it up onto the pads and hold it down with two stainless angles into the deck joists. The doors hang from the Cowdroy Quick-Fix set from Bunnings: two wheel brackets on each door's top edge and a nylon floor guide where they cross.

Build steps

Press View to see a step highlighted in the model.

  1. Confirm before cutting

    Confirm the washer and dryer models and their stacking kit (heat-pump or condenser dryer). Buy the Cowdroy Quick-Fix set from Bunnings first and check its track height (30) and the gap between the two door lines (about 26) against the plans. Measure the power point position along the studio wall and the gaps to the slat screen and studio wall.

  2. Pick-up check

    Eight parts: C1-C6 in 17 mm, D1 and D2 in 19 mm. Each is labelled with its code and an arrow to its top and front on the inside face. Check the 3.5 mm screw holes are drilled through the top (C4) and both backs (C5, C6), the six louvre openings in each back and the two lead holes. Keep the sheets flat and dry until assembly.

  3. Seal, spray and print

    Two coats of exterior edge sealer on every cut edge, door bottoms and tops most of all. Etch-prime and spray the Quick-Fix track satin black. Print the ASA parts. Cut a 740 spacer from an offcut for setting the bays.

  4. Pads on the deck

    Mark the footprint. Bed the thirteen 4 mm ASA edge pads in sealant, one 10g x 25 each: three under each cheek, three under the divider, four under the backs.

  5. Track, then the verticals, upside down

    Lay the top film-face down on blankets on a flat floor or trestles, housings up. Screw the black track into the underside first, front face in line with the front edge: it is easy now and overhead later. Run PU sealant in the cheek and divider housings and stand C1, C2 and C3 in them, inside faces in. Set both bays with the 740 spacer and tack a batten across the free ends to hold them.

  6. Backs: this squares the box

    Run sealant along the rear edges and the back rebate. Stand C5 in the rebate, flush with the kitchen cheek's outside face, then C6 butted against it. Through each CNC hole in the backs, drill a 3 mm pilot about 30 deep into the edge behind, then drive a 10g x 45 stainless decking screw until the head sits just flush. The heads end up in neat rows on the back, facing the slat screen. Fit the black joint angle inside the laundry bay (C6 to the divider), then push a louvre insert into each opening and staple mesh across the inside.

  7. Top screws

    Two people: tip the box onto its back on blankets so the top is at bench height. Pilot through each hole in the top into the edge below (cheeks, divider, backs) and screw as before. All of these heads sit on top of the cabinet, 1985 up, where nobody sees them.

  8. Stand it up and hold it down

    Two people: stand the box upright and walk it onto the pads. Check plumb and the diagonals. Screw the two stainless hold-down angles inside the cheeks into the deck joists and the cheeks. Remove the batten.

  9. Doors and pulls

    Screw the walnut pulls on from behind (left edge of D1, right edge of D2, 850-1250 up). Screw two Quick-Fix wheel brackets to the top edge of each door, hang D2 on the rear line first, then D1 on the front, and adjust to 10 off the deck with D1 flush with the cheeks. Fit a stop so D1 parks 40 short of the studio cheek.

  10. Floor guide

    Screw the Quick-Fix nylon floor guide to the deck where the doors cross (about 765 from the kitchen side). Run both doors end to end.

  11. Grommets and leads

    Fit the split grommets in the two low lead holes (160 above the deck) and run each original lead separately to the power point. No powerboard, nothing in a door track or under a machine foot.

  12. Appliances in

    Roll the fridge in, door hinge on the left, 107 back from the front. Stand the washer, fit the maker's stacking kit and lift the dryer on. Level all three on their own feet; the cabinet carries none of them.

Open items before the cut

Washer and dryer modelsProvisional 600 x 680 x 850 and 600 x 670 x 850. Confirm both models and the maker's stacking kit; the dryer must be heat-pump or condenser.
Dryer airRun the dryer with the laundry door slid open. Makers say a cupboard dryer needs louvred door openings or the door open while it runs (LG asks for two door openings of 387 cm2). The back vents help but are not a substitute.
Quick-Fix geometryThe track height (30) and the gap between the two door lines (about 26) are taken from Cowdroy's published figures. Buy the set first and check both against the plans before the doors and the divider position are released.
Hardware outdoorsQuick-Fix is sold for indoor wardrobes. Under cover it should be fine; spray the wheels with dry PTFE lube once a year and keep the track clear of leaves. Replacement wheel sets are cheap and stocked.
Power pointAbout 100 above the deck on the studio wall; the leads now leave through 60 mm holes 160 above the deck. Measure its position along the wall. An outdoor outlet must be weatherproof (IP-rated).
Rear door set-backWith bypass doors one door has to run behind the other: the laundry door sits about 26 behind the flush fridge door.
Door flatness1916 tall formply doors rely on their balanced film faces to stay flat. Keep both faces sealed; if one bows, a black aluminium angle screwed up its back edge fixes it.
Washer doorWith the laundry door open, the parked door sits just left of the washer and 60 to 80 back from its face. A left-hinged washer door opened past about 120 degrees will touch it. Prefer a right-hinged washer, or open it to 90 degrees.
Site measureFootprint, the 201 gap to the slat screen and the 195 gap to the studio wall come from the handover setout. Measure before the cut is released.
Fridge doorShown left-hinged. If it is re-hung to the right, re-check the swing: it would open towards the divider.
