What it is

An outdoor cabinet on the deck: a fridge in one bay and a stacked washer and dryer in the other. It reads as a plain black box. The top is flush, the fridge door is flush with the sides and runs from 10 mm off the deck to 3 mm under the top, and there are no vents on the front. Two short American walnut pulls, cut from workshop offcuts, are the only detail. Every panel is black film-faced formply cut as a flat-pack kit on a CNC router.

Air: what each appliance needs

Fridge: yes, it needs vents. Its condenser is on the back, so it needs air in low and out high, rising past the coils like a chimney. Fridge makers ask for at least 200 cm² of opening at the bottom and at the top when a fridge is built into cabinetry, and the HRBM292S manual asks for 50 mm at the sides and back and 100 mm above. This cabinet gives 72 mm at each side, 186 mm behind and 183 mm above. Each bay has two 210 × 100 openings low and two high in the back panel. That is 420 cm² gross per level, or about 230 cm² once the louvre and mesh are allowed for, plus the 10 mm gap under the doors. The back faces the slat screen, so none of it shows.

Dryer: run it with the door open. Heat-pump and condenser dryers recycle the air around them, so in a closed cupboard they heat their own intake air and slow down. Makers either say not to put them in a cupboard or ask for large louvred openings in the cupboard door (LG asks for two of 387 cm², top and bottom). Without front vents the simple answer is to slide the laundry door open while the dryer runs. It parks over the fridge, so nothing gets in the way. The back louvres let the bay air out after a run.

Washer: no vents needed while it runs. It makes little heat. Leave its door ajar afterwards so the drum dries; the back louvres let the damp out of the bay.

Weather and fixing

The deck is about a metre off the ground under cover, so the box can sit on the deck. It stands on 4 mm ASA pads you can't see from standing height, so water drains out under the ply edges and the deck boards can dry. Every cut edge, including the top's four exposed edges and the door grooves, gets two coats of sealer. Two stainless angles inside the cheeks screw into the deck joists to hold the box down in wind.

How it goes together

The top is routed underneath with 6 mm housings for every vertical. You build the carcass upside down on the top, so the housings square it up. Then you stand it on the pads, screw up the two tracks and hang the doors. The doors hang from face-fixed hangers, so each one runs up in front of its own track and hides it. A small ASA guide under the crossover rides in a groove in each door's bottom edge, so nothing shows above the deck.

Build steps

Press View to see a step highlighted in the model.

  1. Confirm before cutting

    Confirm the washer and dryer models and their stacking kit (dryer must be heat-pump or condenser). Choose the top-hung track kit (face-fixed hangers, two tracks under the top). Electrician confirms three outlets on the studio wall. Measure the deck and the gaps to the slat screen and the studio wall. Release the two HOLD lead holes, or leave them off the cut.

  2. Seal, rout and print

    Seal every CNC-cut edge with two coats of exterior edge sealer, bottom edges most of all. Rout the 8 x 12 guide groove along the bottom edge of each door and seal it. Print the ASA parts (Prints tab).

  3. Pads on the deck

    Mark the footprint. Bed the thirteen 4 mm ASA edge pads in sealant on the lines of the cheeks, divider and backs, one 10g x 25 each.

  4. Carcass on the top, upside down

    Lay the top film-face down on padded trestles, housings up. Run PU sealant in each housing. Stand C1, C2, C3, C5 and C6 in their housings; the housings square the box. Screw down through the top into each edge, 8g x 50 stainless at 200 centres, countersunk and sealed.

  5. Angles and louvres

    Fit the four black aluminium angles inside the back corners. Staple stainless mesh behind the eight back openings, then bed and screw an ASA louvre over each one from the outside, blades sloping down and out.

  6. Stand it up and hold it down

    Two people. Turn the carcass upright onto the pads. Check plumb and diagonals. Screw the two stainless hold-down angles inside the cheeks into the deck joists and the cheeks.

  7. Tracks

    Cut both top-hung tracks to 1497 and screw them up into the top: front track 19-42 back from the front edge, rear track 63-86.

  8. Doors and pulls

    Screw the walnut pulls on from behind: on the left edge of D1 and the right edge of D2, 850-1250 above the deck. Fit the face-fixed hangers and hang D1 on the front track and D2 on the rear. Set D1 flush with the cheek fronts, 10 off the deck and 3 under the top.

  9. Floor guide

    Close both doors. Screw the ASA guide to the deck under the crossover so its fins sit in the door grooves; run both doors end to end.

  10. Grommets and leads

    Only after the HOLD is released: fit the split grommets in the lead holes and route each original lead separately to the wall. No powerboard, nothing in a door track or under a machine foot.

  11. Appliances in

    Roll the fridge in, door hinge on the left, 107 back from the cabinet front. Stand the washer, fit the maker's stacking kit and lift the dryer on. Level all three on their own feet; the cabinet carries none of them.

Open items before the cut

Washer and dryer modelsProvisional 600 x 680 x 850 and 600 x 670 x 850. Confirm both models and the maker's stacking kit; the dryer must be heat-pump or condenser.
Dryer airRun the dryer with the laundry door slid open. Makers say a cupboard dryer needs louvred door openings or the door open while it runs (LG asks for two door openings of 387 cm2). The back louvres help but are not a substitute.
PowerOwner says there is a power point on the studio wall. Position, outlet count and weather cover are unmeasured. The two lead holes are HOLD until an electrician confirms.
Track kitNeeds face-fixed hangers so each door can run up in front of its own track. Each track must fit about 23 deep x 40 high under the top; the rear door clears the front track by only 2 mm on these numbers, so check the chosen kit's drawing.
Front track showsOver the laundry half the front track sits in front of the set-back laundry door, so a 40 mm strip of it shows under the top. Specify black anodised track so it reads as a shadow line.
Rear door set-backWith bypass doors one door has to run behind the other: the laundry door sits 44 behind the flush fridge door. A fully flush front needs in-line flush-sliding hardware (both doors close in one plane); possible, but dearer and mostly rated for indoor use.
Door flatnessWithout full-height stiles, 1955 tall formply doors rely on the balanced film faces to stay flat. Keep both faces sealed; if one bows, a black aluminium angle screwed up the back edge fixes it.
Site measureFootprint, the 201 gap to the slat screen and the 195 gap to the studio wall come from the handover setout. Measure before the cut is released.
Washer doorWith the laundry door open, the parked door sits just left of the washer and 60 to 100 back from its face. A left-hinged washer door opened past about 120 degrees will touch it. Prefer a right-hinged washer, or open it to 90 degrees.
Fridge doorShown left-hinged. If it is re-hung to the right, re-check the swing: it would open towards the divider.
