What it is

An outdoor cabinet on the deck: a fridge in one bay and a stacked washer and dryer in the other. It reads as a plain black box. The top is flush; the fridge door, the sides and a slim fascia under the top all sit in one plane, with only a 3 mm shadow line above the door. There are no vents on the front. Two short American walnut pulls, cut from workshop offcuts, are the only detail. Every panel is black film-faced formply cut as a flat-pack kit on a CNC router.

Air: what each appliance needs

Fridge: yes, it needs vents. Its condenser is on the back, so it needs air in low and out high, rising past the coils like a chimney. Fridge makers ask for at least 200 cm² of opening at the bottom and at the top when a fridge is built into cabinetry, and the HRBM292S manual asks for 50 mm at the sides and back and 100 mm above. This cabinet gives 72 mm at each side, 186 mm behind and 183 mm above. Each bay has two 210 × 100 openings low and two high in the back panel: 420 cm² per level, about 290 cm² once the mesh is allowed for, plus the 10 mm gap under the doors. The back faces the slat screen, so none of it shows.

Dryer: run it with the door open. Heat-pump and condenser dryers recycle the air around them, so in a closed cupboard they heat their own intake air and slow down. Makers either say not to put them in a cupboard or ask for large louvred openings in the cupboard door (LG asks for two of 387 cm², top and bottom). Without front vents the simple answer is to slide the laundry door open while the dryer runs. It parks over the fridge, so nothing gets in the way. The back vents let the bay air out after a run.

Washer: no vents needed while it runs. It makes little heat. Leave its door ajar afterwards so the drum dries; the back vents let the damp out of the bay.

Keeping storm water out of the vents

Each back opening sits under a printed ASA rain hood. The hood is closed on top, both sides and the face, and open only at the bottom. Its mouth faces the deck, 80 mm below the bottom of the opening. Wind-driven rain hitting the back has to blow upwards 80 mm inside the hood before it can reach the opening, which is how standard weather cowls on wall vents work. A drip lip inside the mouth, a sealant bead along the top of each hood and stainless mesh behind the opening complete it. The back also faces the slat screen, 201 mm away, under cover. The lowest hood mouth is 30 mm above the deck.

Weather and fixing

The deck is about a metre off the ground under cover, so the box can sit on the deck. It stands on 4 mm ASA pads you can't see from standing height, so water drains out under the ply edges and the deck boards can dry. Every cut edge, including the top's four exposed edges and the door grooves, gets two coats of sealer. Two stainless angles inside the cheeks screw into the deck joists to hold the box down in wind.

How it goes together

The top is routed underneath with 6 mm housings for every vertical. You build the carcass upside down on the top, so the housings square it up. Then you stand it on the pads, fit the fascia and the Brio track behind it, and hang the doors. Nothing is printed in the door gear: it is an off-the-shelf Brio Wardrobe Double 50 kit (WD50-15/2) with face-fixed hangers on the back of each door, door stops, and 101N floor guides that run in a sawcut groove in each door's bottom edge, so nothing shows above the deck.

Build steps

Press View to see a step highlighted in the model.

  1. Confirm before cutting

    Confirm the washer and dryer models and their stacking kit (dryer must be heat-pump or condenser). Buy the Brio WD50-15/2 kit and check its track against data sheet B55 before the fascia and door heights are released. Measure the power point position along the studio wall (it is about 100 above the deck) and the gaps to the slat screen and studio wall.

  2. Seal, groove and print

    Seal every CNC-cut edge with two coats of exterior edge sealer, bottom edges most of all. Cut the 5 x 20 sawcut groove along the bottom edge of each door for the Brio guide, and seal it. Print the ASA parts (Prints tab).

  3. Pads on the deck

    Mark the footprint. Bed the thirteen 4 mm ASA edge pads in sealant on the lines of the cheeks, divider and backs, one 10g x 25 each.

  4. Carcass on the top, upside down

    Lay the top film-face down on padded trestles, housings up. Run PU sealant in each housing. Stand C1, C2, C3, C5 and C6 in their housings; the housings square the box. Screw down through the top into each edge, 8g x 50 stainless at 200 centres, countersunk and sealed.

  5. Angles and rain hoods

    Fit the four black aluminium angles inside the back corners. Staple stainless mesh behind the eight back openings, then bed each ASA rain hood in sealant over its opening from the outside, mouth down, 4 x 6g x 16. Run a bead along the top edge of each hood.

  6. Stand it up and hold it down

    Two people. Turn the carcass upright onto the pads. Check plumb and diagonals. Screw the two stainless hold-down angles inside the cheeks into the deck joists and the cheeks.

  7. Fascia and track

    Push the fascia up into its housing between the cheeks, flush with their fronts, and screw down through the top. Cut the Brio double track to 1497 and screw it up into the top behind the fascia, set out to the Brio data sheet so the fridge door hangs flush with the cheeks.

  8. Doors and pulls

    Screw the walnut pulls on from behind: on the left edge of D1 and the right edge of D2, 850-1250 above the deck. Screw the Brio face-fixed hangers to the back face of each door, hang D1 on the front line and D2 on the rear, and adjust to 10 off the deck with a 3 mm line under the fascia. Set the Brio door stop so D1 parks 40 short of the studio cheek.

  9. Floor guides

    Screw the two Brio 101N guides to the deck where each door always passes over them (about 750 and 800 from the kitchen side), engaged in the door grooves. Run both doors end to end.

  10. Grommets and leads

    Fit the split grommets in the two low lead holes (160 above the deck) and run each original lead separately to the power point. No powerboard, nothing in a door track or under a machine foot.

  11. Appliances in

    Roll the fridge in, door hinge on the left, 107 back from the cabinet front. Stand the washer, fit the maker's stacking kit and lift the dryer on. Level all three on their own feet; the cabinet carries none of them.

Open items before the cut

Washer and dryer modelsProvisional 600 x 680 x 850 and 600 x 670 x 850. Confirm both models and the maker's stacking kit; the dryer must be heat-pump or condenser.
Dryer airRun the dryer with the laundry door slid open. Makers say a cupboard dryer needs louvred door openings or the door open while it runs (LG asks for two door openings of 387 cm2). The back vents help but are not a substitute.
Brio track geometryDoor lines, track set-out and the 46 mm fascia are modelled on Brio's published figures (panel height = opening - 53, 52 wide track, about 26 between door lines). Check the kit's data sheet B55 before releasing the fascia and door heights.
Hardware outdoorsBrio Wardrobe Double 50 is sold for wardrobes. Under cover it should be fine; spray the bearings with dry PTFE lube once a year and keep the track clear of leaves.
Power pointAbout 100 above the deck on the studio wall; the leads now leave through 60 mm holes 160 above the deck. Measure its position along the wall. An outdoor outlet must be weatherproof (IP-rated).
Rear door set-backWith bypass doors one door has to run behind the other: the laundry door sits 26 behind the flush fridge door.
Door flatness1915 tall formply doors rely on their balanced film faces to stay flat. Keep both faces sealed; if one bows, a black aluminium angle screwed up its back edge fixes it.
Washer doorWith the laundry door open, the parked door sits just left of the washer and 60 to 80 back from its face. A left-hinged washer door opened past about 120 degrees will touch it. Prefer a right-hinged washer, or open it to 90 degrees.
Site measureFootprint, the 201 gap to the slat screen and the 195 gap to the studio wall come from the handover setout. Measure before the cut is released.
Fridge doorShown left-hinged. If it is re-hung to the right, re-check the swing: it would open towards the divider.
